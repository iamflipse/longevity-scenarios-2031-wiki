# The Three 2031 Scenarios

David Wood frames 2031 as **three co-existing credible futures** for the longevity community and longevity science. None is declared "most likely" — all are broadly feasible.

## 1. The abundant future of longevity

- People live **longer and healthily**, and this is **open to everyone**.
- People can regularly take **"deep detox" treatments** that undo the damage of the last few years of aging, allowing all-round rejuvenation of mind and body.

**Supporting trends cited at the Madrid summit:** aging proven biologically malleable in many species; remarkable results from partial **epigenetic reprogramming**; selective removal of **senescent cells** (senolytics); **CRISPR** gene editing already in approved medical therapies and moving toward aging mechanisms; improved **biological aging measurement** (organ-level biological age; biosynthetic organ replacement becoming less fanciful); **organoid tissue models**; **combination treatments** (aging is multi-pronged — many damage types coexist, so combinations will be needed).

## 2. The frail future of longevity

- **Science rejected**: public backlash against the medical community, conspiracy theories, quack science, "frankly terrible" lifestyles.
- **Evidence of stagnation:**
  - Decades of encouraging signals, yet **no new intervention extending maximum human longevity** beyond what it has been for decades.
  - Encouraging **mouse lifespan results** have not produced human rejuvenation; many once-exciting experiments **proved hard to replicate**.
  - **Longevity hype** in the marketplace: many products have little real evidence beyond "wishful thinking."
  - Healthspan gains are **unevenly distributed** → growing **longevity gap** and **healthspan gap**.
  - Biological-age measurements correlate with lifespan but uncertainty remains: a treatment may make someone "seem younger" while having adverse effects elsewhere — they could even die sooner.
  - Aging is **extraordinarily multi-causal**: reversing individual hallmarks may not affect the underlying accumulation of overall biological damage.
  - Endless **debates** over aging theories, causes, and how to fix it; funding "comes but sometimes goes."
  - **Conspiratorial thinking** in the wider community threatens science and medicine; even powerful countries' health departments sometimes spout unscientific theories.
- **Core risk:** rejuvenation tech makes some aspects of life healthier while **society as a whole becomes less healthy faster**.

## 3. The mediocre future

- **Some improvements**: new treatments add months (occasionally years) for chronic diseases of aging.
- Therapies are **expensive**, but a competitive marketplace plus **proactive government policy** keeps prices from rising too high.
- **Regulatory paralysis** contributes to **research stagnation**; ongoing debates over which therapies have the most promise.
- **Conservative bioethicists** insist on "focus on disease, not aging."
- Talented researchers **leave the field** for more financially rewarding (but socially destabilizing) alternative technologies.
- Wood's punchline: in 2031 he may be back at the same conference "having much the same debates and discussions as now. A mediocre future indeed."

**The point of the exercise:** all three are credible; what matters is finding the **levers** that shift their probabilities (see [The Ten Levers](ten-levers.md)).
