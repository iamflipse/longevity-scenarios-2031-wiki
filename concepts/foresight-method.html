The Science of Foresight

Wood opens with the crystal-ball picture and asks: what's most significant about it? The levers scientists, activists, and concerned citizens have found — because better foresight both prepares us for risks/opportunities and gives leverage, control, and steering over how the future unfolds.

ABCD of trends

Foresight = close observation + intelligent extrapolation of existing trends (micro-trends today → macro-trends later). The ABCD considerations:

  • Accelerate: trends may go faster than expected.
  • Break: trends may go slower / break.
  • Combine/converge: trends may combine, converge, change their nature, and disrupt many other aspects of life. (Wood groups these as the "ABCDs.")

EFGH of trends

How we act on trends:

  • Engage with them.
  • Force them a bit faster when they're very positive.
  • Guide them more generally.
  • Harness them for truly beneficial purposes.

Caveats from the talk: predicting the future is hard in a time of geopolitical uncertainty, regional volatility, and rapid AI/tech news; the Overton window moves fast. Both an abundant future (vitality, creativity, freedom) and a frail future (alienation, exhaustion, conflict, oppression, surveillance) coexist as credible visions — see Three scenarios.
