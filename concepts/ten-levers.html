The Ten Levers

Wood presents ten trends-and-levers that can be engaged to change the probabilities of the three futures. They derive from his own research and from the three-volume Springer series Frontiers of Longevity Science (he was one of four co-editors; ~1,700 pages, 100+ authors; e-book October, soft copy November 2026).

#LeverEssence
1More intelligenceAttract more people into longevity research and stop talented researchers leaving; use AI co-scientists; beware misaligned intelligence — one-track minds, unexamined biases, "clever noise"; (also: misaligned AGI "could kill all of us faster than aging")
2Deeper collaborationBest intelligence comes from collaboration — "the Moon wasn't one man's insight, it was millions of people"; cross-pollinate different styles of intelligence; remove barriers: language/terminology/standard mismatches, outdated IP licensing, perverse publication incentives, adversarial team competition
3Nimbler regulationTechnology outpaces regulators; balance risk of approving poor-efficacy treatments against the risk of delaying the treatments that could bring the biggest health gains; support conditional approvals, personalized/group-specific approvals (e.g. by gene combination), and agreed surrogate endpoints ("proxies") so treatments can progress
4Finer toolsHistory shows tools drive breakthroughs (better microscopes + synthetic chemistry solved infections); today's waves: DNA analysis & synthesis; ahead: nanoscale assembly and control (already in some treatments), AI analysis/models, organoid models, mRNA vaccines
5Better dataAI is only as good as its training/operational data. ImageNet (Hinton, Krizhevsky, Sutskever, 2012) depended on Fei-Fei Li's Stanford dataset + Amazon Mechanical Turk labeling; AlphaFold (Nobel Prize) depended on 50 years of PDB protein-structure data. The most critical experiments now: combinatorial damage-repair — multiple simultaneous treatments in animals, measuring organs/tissues and lifespans, so AI gets data it can progress with
6Accelerated fundingDiscretionary money sits idle: government budgets, pension-trustee funds, philanthropists/billionaires (in most cases spending little or nothing on longevity), and very successful listed companies. Encourage all to fund by scientific insight rather than "existing social dogmas, vested interests, and safe conservative funding patterns"; support directions that "initially may appear quirky"
7Higher integrityToo much tolerance of poor reporting and wishful thinking risks a "hype lash" (the longevity equivalent of the "tech lash"); fix: pre-registration of tests, full documentation for replication, reporting results against what was hoped, and setting aside ideological/political drives
8Greater opennessOne-track intelligences cling to ideas; outdated hypotheses persist too long, amplified by social media and political pressures; gatekeepers morph into blockers of novelty ("not going to fund this new stuff"); use decentralized approaches to work around them; build emotional resilience to tolerate unknowns, failures, and unexpected surprises
9Stronger moral clarityA "moral haze": many are frightened by the possibility of significantly extended lifespans — "aging is natural, we should accept decline." Wood: people are "being killed by bad philosophy" (so-called bioethicists) whose views get embedded in policy defaults and cultural narratives. Widespread rejuvenation is "a great humanitarian goal," a fundamentally moral purpose. His 30-principle framework covers purpose, evidence, governance, caution, underpinned by two premises: F1 — pay serious attention to the transformative potential of emerging technologies; F2 — pay serious attention to the deep social factors behind flourishing (culture, institutions, shared worldviews). Full essay on dw2blog.com ("vital foundation")
10Head-turning breakthroughsArguably the most important lever: results that can't be denied — animals living significantly longer and remaining vibrant and healthy throughout (see LEV Foundation & RMR). That news spreads virally through experts, journalists, and social leaders → the public demands "a war on aging" → funding unlocks → a "race to the top" toward longevity escape velocity

Structural note: levers 1–5 depend on accelerated funding; unlocking that funding requires levers 7–9 (integrity, openness, moral clarity); lever 10 is the breakthrough that triggers the cascade.
